▼ The 1:1 gap is the #1 onboarding checklist gap at every depth — and it widens with tenure: 50% → 52% → 63%

154 people at the 30-day check, 136 at 60-day and 140 at 90-day reported no high-quality one-on-one time with their direct leadership. It outranks every other checklist item at all three depths, and the 13-point rise from 30 to 90 days means the gap is not closing as people settle in — it is growing. Entirely within manager control.

● Development conversations are the softest favorability item at every depth

“Discussed development goals with direct leadership” sits at 55.5% on the 60-day check and 63.2% on the 90-day — the lowest-scoring sub-item in both surveys. Paired with the 1:1 gap above, both of the weakest signals in this look-back are manager-coaching behaviors.

▲ Favorability is highest at the shallowest look-back and steps down with depth: 85.8% → 84.6% → 83.3%

The 30-day check reads strongest, easing 2.5 points by the 90-day check. Response rate follows the same gradient (39.2% → 34.6% → 33.0%), so the deeper checkpoints rest on smaller, later-tenure samples.

▼ The gap widens with tenure instead of closing: 50% → 52% → 63%

If 1:1s were simply slow to start, the gap would shrink as people reach 90 days. It does the opposite — 13 points worse at the 90-day check than the 30-day. Nearly two in three people at 90 days report no quality one-on-one time with their leader.

● Leadership favorability holds up even where 1:1s are missing

“Direct leadership is easily accessible” scores 79.9% at 60 days and 79.3% at 90 — people can reach their managers. What is missing is the structured, scheduled one-on-one, not approachability. That makes this a cadence problem rather than a relationship problem, and cheaper to fix.
