Key Call-Outs
19%
Nearly one scored hire in five is a weak JD fit
Across 629 scored 2026 hires — 78% of the 808 core-role non-referral hires this year — the average resume evidences 64.3% of its job description’s weighted requirements. 118 (19%) fall below 50% and only 110 (17%) reach 80%. It does not look seasonal: across the seven months with usable coverage the mean stays inside a 62.0–69.9% band. The variation that matters is between roles, not between months or recruiters — CSR to PCC spans 22 points, about 1.5× the widest recruiter gap once role mix is held constant (14 points).
53.3%
Patient Care Coordinator is the weakest role
Only 2 of 40 PCC hires are strong fits and 19 score below 50%. ACE is statistically alongside it at 54.2% but on only 11 resumes; the next role up sits 9 points clear. 3 of its nine requirements are evidenced by fewer than a quarter of hires and 5 by a quarter or fewer; “contacting physicians for missing information” appears in 1 resume of 40. Three readings fit equally: the JD describes work these hires have not done, the JD describes the job inaccurately, or PCC screening is not selecting against the JD at all. Worth resolving before the next intake — start by asking a PCC supervisor which of the nine requirements the job actually involves.
40 of 152
pharmacy techs evidence no licence or certification
Pharmacy Technician is the second-largest role at 257 hires (behind PAS at 368). Of 152 scored, 148 show pharmacy experience but only 89 a state licence and 61 a national certification — 40 (26%) evidence neither. The JD requires Kansas registration at hire and national licensure within six months, so this is compliance-relevant. PT also has the thinnest coverage of any role at 64%, so verify against licence records before acting.
75.5%
CSR is the one role clearly working
CSR scores 75.5% on 99 resumes with 44 strong fits and just 3 weak — the only role above 70%, and the lowest of its 6 heavily-weighted requirements sits at 73%. Two light requirements do lag — adverse-event awareness and “some college”, together 7% of the weight, are evidenced by about a third of hires. Its heaviest requirement, customer-service or call-centre experience, is evidenced by 98% of hires. That is the profile to aim the other five roles at: requirements the market actually supplies, evidenced consistently rather than in patches.
Methodology. Match % = the share of weighted JD requirements evidenced in the resume. Each of the six job descriptions was parsed into nine weighted requirement dimensions drawn from its REQUIRED QUALIFICATIONS and KEY RESPONSIBILITIES; weights reflect how central each line is to the role rather than whether the JD words it as required or preferred, and each role’s weights sum to 1.00. A dimension scores 0.85 when the requirement is directly evidenced (1.00 when it recurs), 0.45–0.55 on adjacent evidence only, 0.00 otherwise; dimensions specifying a tenure (“3+ years”, “2–4 years”) are capped at 0.70 without an explicit tenure statement.

Population and sources. 986 of 1221 2026 hire records fall in the six core roles, 808 of them non-referral; supervisor, manager and team-lead titles are excluded, job-family labels such as PAS “Case Manager” are kept. Resumes came from three sources: the 1,319-file recruiting library, the 272 files recruiters supplied in September (from 330 supplied, one folder of 57 corporate resumes set aside and one duplicate removed), and 5 individual resumes supplied since — 2 of which belong to core-role non-referral hires, one of them replacing a scan that had returned no text. A sixth upload was set aside: its text is identical to a resume already scored for a different hire whose name differs by one letter, so attributing it would have invented evidence; the upload’s nominal hire, a Patient Care Coordinator, remains unscored pending confirmation from TA. Each was matched to a named hire by a first-name-plus-surname token alignment test, then one resume kept per hire, longer text winning. Two matchers were used — the library files through a test with an acceptance floor of 78, the September files through an earlier variant with no floor — so every September file was force-assigned; the lowest confidence among scored resumes was 89.7 for September and 86.7 for the library; across all accepted matches, including unscored non-core hires, the floor was 76.6 and 86.7. The lowest-confidence matches in both sets were read by hand and none proved to be the wrong person. That yields 972 matched hires, 785 in the six core roles, of which 12 are scanned PDFs that returned no usable text under OCR within the time available and were dropped — leaving 629 non-referral (469 library, 158 September, 2 later uploads) and 144 referral resumes scored (81% of core referral hires). The other 480 library files are applicant-pool resumes for which the matcher found no 2026 hire; a hand sample confirmed these are candidates who were not hired, though a handful of unusual surnames may be genuine misses.

Coverage is the main limit on this view. 629 of 808 non-referral core hires (78%) have a scorable resume, so 179 do not, and the shortfall is not random — it clusters in specific cuts: Yolanda (36% of 134) and Sabrina (15% of 13) by recruiter, Pharmacy Technician (64%) by role, and June and July (52% and 41%) by month. Figures for those cuts are directional only, and the ±1.3 beside the headline is a within-sample standard error, not a margin for the 179 unscored hires. The two main sources also score differently — library resumes average 63.5% and September-supplied ones 66.8% — so the unscored hires should not be assumed to look like the scored ones. The 808 denominator counts hire records; collapsing 30 repeat rows for the same person gives 778 people and coverage of 81%. Separately, the workbook’s row-level Resume Status column claims 1,192 of 1221 hires have a resume on file while its own Sept-09 per-recruiter headers say 771 and this exercise located 972; the row-level column appears to have been bulk-updated and should not be used for recordkeeping assurance.

June 2026 review, for context only. That run scored 760 resumes at 68.0% overall (CSR 73.5% on 82 · ACE 70.2% on 39 · AAA 70.0% on 65 · PT 69.9% on 162 · PAS 66.0% on 369 · PCC 63.7% on 43). Those figures are deliberately kept off the panels above and no delta is shown, because they cannot be compared: June’s PAS count (369) exceeds this workbook’s entire PAS population including referrals (368), its ACE count (39) exceeds ACE’s (22), its rubrics were built independently from the same job descriptions, and the 22 individuals it excluded at TA’s request could not be re-applied here. Any apparent movement would mix a rubric change with a population change.

Three residual cautions. (1) 29 scored resumes arrived as Indeed “Smart Screening” PDFs wrapping the resume in AI-written text that paraphrases the job description, 16 of them with an AI phone-screen transcript attached; both blocks were cut and only the resume body read. After cutting they average 65.7% against 64.3% for plain resumes. (2) The score rewards resume detail: match % correlates with resume length at r = 0.28, so a terse resume from a well-qualified hire under-scores. Treat any individual result below 50% as a prompt to read the resume, not a verdict. (3) 9 resumes are filed under a posting that differs from the hire’s workbook title and are scored against the workbook title; excluding them moves the headline to 64.4% on 620.
